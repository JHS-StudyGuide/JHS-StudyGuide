Plot, Characterization, Rhyme, Meter, and More

A readable review page for key literary and drama terms, written in simple language with clear examples of what each term means.

Plot Spectacle Dialogue Music Characterization Rhyme & Meter

Plot

Plot is the sequence of events in a story. It is the backbone of the story and shows how the action develops from beginning to end.

Basic Structure

Exposition

The beginning of the story. It introduces the characters, setting, and situation.

Basic Structure

Rising Action

The conflict starts to build here. Events become more tense and lead toward the climax.

Basic Structure

Climax

The most exciting or important part of the story. It is the turning point where the main conflict reaches its peak.

Basic Structure

Falling Action

This happens after the climax. The story starts to slow down as the conflict begins to resolve.

Basic Structure

Resolution

The ending of the story. It shows how the conflict is solved or how the story closes.

Plot Structure

Parallel Plot

Two storylines happen at the same time. The plots may connect or affect each other.

Plot Structure

Episodic Plot

The story is made of separate events or episodes. Each part can stand on its own, but they still belong to one story.

Spectacle

Spectacle is everything the audience sees in a performance. It is visual only and helps create the mood and style of the story.

Costumes

These show a character’s role, personality, or time period. They help the audience understand the story visually.

Facial Expression

These are the emotions shown on a character’s face. They help express feelings without words.

Gestures

These are movements made by the hands, arms, or body. They help show emotion and meaning.

Props / Security

Props are objects used by actors in a performance. They make the scene more realistic and support the action.

Dialogue

Dialogue is the conversation between characters. It moves the story forward and helps the audience understand what is happening.

Reveals

Character Traits

Dialogue shows what kind of person a character is. It can reveal personality, attitude, or values.

Reveals

Conflict

Dialogue can show disagreements or tension between characters. This helps build drama in the story.

Reveals

Feelings

Characters often express emotions through dialogue. This helps the audience understand how they feel.

Reveals

Plot Development

Dialogue can give important information or create new events. It helps the story move from one scene to the next.

Music

Music creates emotion and sets the mood of a scene. It helps the audience feel the atmosphere of the story.

Includes

Background Music

This is music played quietly in the background. It supports the scene without taking attention away from the action.

Includes

Instrumental Music

This is music without singing or lyrics. It is often used to build mood and emotion.

Includes

Sound Effects

These are added sounds that make a scene more realistic. They can also make moments feel more dramatic or exciting.

Characterization

Characterization is how an author describes or develops a character in a story. It helps the reader understand what the character is like.

Type

Direct Characterization

The author clearly tells the reader what a character is like. The traits are stated openly.

Type

Indirect Characterization

The author shows the character’s traits through behavior, speech, and actions. The reader infers what the character is like.

Techniques of indirect characterization: dialogue, actions and behavior, physical appearance, thoughts and inner monologue, reactions and interactions, and backstory and background.

Rhyme & Meter

Rhyme and meter help give poetry its sound, rhythm, and musical quality. They are important in understanding how poems are built.

Rhyme

Meaning

Rhyme is the repetition of similar ending sounds in words. It is often used to make poems sound musical and memorable.

Rhyme Types

End Rhyme

This happens when the last words in lines sound alike. It is the most common type of rhyme in poetry.

Rhyme Types

Internal Rhyme

This happens when words rhyme within the same line of poetry. It creates a faster, more musical sound.

Rhyme Types

Slant Rhyme

This is when words almost rhyme, but not exactly. The sounds are similar, but they do not fully match.

Rhyme Scheme

Pattern of Rhymes

Rhyme scheme is the pattern of rhyming words in a poem. It is shown using letters such as AABB, ABAB, or ABCC.

Rhyme Scheme

Couplets

Couplets follow an AABB pattern. Two lines rhyme in a row, then the next two lines rhyme with each other.

Rhyme Scheme

Alternating

Alternating rhyme follows an ABAB pattern. Every other line rhymes, creating a back-and-forth sound.

Rhyme Scheme

Other Patterns

Some poems use other rhyme patterns, such as ABCC. The letters simply show which lines rhyme with each other.

Meter

Meaning

Meter is the rhythm of a poem. It is created by the pattern of stressed and unstressed syllables.

Meter

Feet

A foot is the smallest unit of meter in poetry. It usually contains two or three syllables arranged in a repeated pattern.

Meter

Common Feet

Iamb: da-DUM, Trochee: DUM-da, Anapest: da-da-DUM, Dactyl: DUM-da-da, Spondee: DUM-DUM, Pyrrhic: da-da.

Poetry Forms

Traditional Poetry and Free Verse

Traditional poetry usually follows a strict rhyme scheme and meter, such as iambic pentameter. Free verse does not follow a consistent meter and gives the poet more freedom.
