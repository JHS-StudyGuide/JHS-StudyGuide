Scarcity, allocation, and the systems built to manage them

A study companion on how societies decide what gets made, how, and for whom — from the classroom paper problem to centuries of economic theory.

01

Allocation (Alokasyon)

Definition

The mechanism used to distribute, assign, and manage scarce or limited resources to address a country's economic problems — deciding how limited resources can satisfy competing economic needs.

02

The Three Fundamental Questions

Every economic decision in a society ultimately answers these:

What?

Which goods and services should be produced.

How?

What methods or processes are used to produce them.

For whom?

Who the target consumers or beneficiaries are.

03

Types of Products

Products are classified by how accessible and contested they are:

TypeDescriptionExamples
Private goods
Pampribadong produkto		 Not easily accessible to everyone — usually available only to those with high income. Also called excludable goods. Vehicles, expensive clothing, gadgets
Club goods
Pangkapisanang produkto		 Not contested or competed over by consumers; usually want-based, created goods. —
Common goods
Karaniwang produkto		 Meet the basic needs or desires of all people; often contested but generally affordable and adequately supplied. —
Public goods
Pampublikong produkto		 Serve the entire household or community; benefit all people. Roads, bridges, public parks
04

Economic Systems

What is an economic system?

A combination of structures and institutions designed to implement allocation methods — organizing production, distribution, and consumption to solve a society's economic problems.

What shapes the choice of system?

Historical development and evolution of ideas, the practical and structural needs of society, available resources and sociopolitical environment, and the economic theories explaining behavior and institutions.

05

Theories That Shaped Economic Systems

TheoryDescriptionKey feature
Feudalism
Piyudalismo		 Power derives from land ownership — controlling large land areas equates to economic and political power. Land-based power
Mercantilism
Merkantilismo		 Values precious metals (gold, silver) as wealth; favors exporting over importing for national growth. Export priority, bullionism
Physiocrats Highlights the importance of nature and free trade in economic development. Nature-centered, laissez-faire
Classical Economics Advocates minimal government intervention; the "invisible hand" naturally regulates supply and demand. Free market adjustment
Neoclassical Economics
Neoklasiko		 Focuses on demand, supply, elasticity, and production as the key economic factors. Demand–supply analysis
Keynesian Economics Sees capitalism as effective but stresses government's role in regulating demand, supply, investment, and pricing. Active government intervention

07

Key Takeaways

  • Allocation addresses scarcity by directing limited resources effectively.
  • Every society answers what, how, and for whom to produce.
  • Products range from exclusive (private, club) to widely shared (common, public).
  • Economic systems organize allocation and are shaped by historical theory.
  • Economic thought moved from land-based power and mercantilism toward free-market and interventionist models.
