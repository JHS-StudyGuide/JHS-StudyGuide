Production, Entrepreneurship, and Business Organizations

A study page about how resources are turned into products and services, how entrepreneurs operate, and how different business organizations are structured.
Main idea Production creates goods and services from resources and helps the economy grow.
Entrepreneurship Entrepreneurs take risks, organize resources, and create new products or services.
Business forms Businesses can be sole proprietorships, partnerships, corporations, OPCs, or cooperatives.

2. Production

Production is the process of creating or transforming resources (inputs) into goods and services (outputs). It is important to a country’s economy because it helps meet people’s needs, creates jobs, improves living standards, and strengthens a country’s ability to compete in the global market.

Land

Natural resources

Refers to natural resources, including everything obtained from nature such as land, forests, mines, and water. The payment for the use of land is rent.

Labor

Human effort

The physical and mental ability of people in production. It may come from workers, professionals like doctors and engineers, or entrepreneurs. The payment for labor is wages or salary.

Capital

Man-made tools

Man-made goods used to produce other goods and services. These may include tools, machines, vehicles, and buildings. The payment for the use of capital is interest.

Entrepreneurship

Organizing business

The ability of an individual to organize and operate a business. The entrepreneur brings together land, labor, and capital to create products and services. The payment for the entrepreneur is profit.

3. Entrepreneurship

An entrepreneur is a risk taker who wants to earn profit and create new products or services. The entrepreneur builds the business, makes decisions, and faces risk in exchange for potential profit.

Schumpeter

Innovation and change

Joseph Schumpeter, an Austrian-American economist, believed the entrepreneur is an innovator who introduces new products, services, or methods of production. He called this creative destruction because new ideas replace old ones and make room for progress.

Jean Baptiste Say

Leader and coordinator

Jean Baptiste Say, a French economist, emphasized the entrepreneur’s role as a leader and coordinator of the factors of production.

5. Types of Business Organizations

These are the main forms of business organizations discussed in the notes.

1. Sole Proprietorship

One owner

Definition: A type of business owned by one individual. The owner has complete control but also carries the greatest responsibility.

  • Easy to establish
  • Unlimited liability; personal property may be used to pay business debts
  • Single ownership
  • Direct control by the owner
  • The owner makes the decisions
  • Easy to start and organize
  • Complete control of the owner
  • All profits belong to the owner
  • Easy to stop operations
  • Limited access to capital
  • The life of the business depends on the owner
  • It may be difficult to raise large funds
2. Partnership

Two or more owners

Definition: A business owned and managed by two or more individuals. They have an agreement on profit sharing and responsibility.

Types:

  • General Partnership: All partners have equal responsibility and management rights.
  • Limited Partnership: One general partner has unlimited liability, while limited partners have limited liability.
  • Partnership at will: No fixed date or period for ending the partnership.
  • Partnership for a fixed term: Has a set date when the agreement ends.
  • Particular Partnership: Formed for a specific project or task only.
  • Universal Partnership of all present property: Includes all the property of the partners.
  • Universal Partnership of profits: The partnership is for profits only.

Common advantages:

  • More capital from combined contributions
  • Easier to establish than a corporation
  • Combined skills and knowledge of the partners

Common disadvantages:

  • Unlimited liability, except in a limited partnership
  • Possible disagreements in decision-making
  • The partnership may end when one partner leaves
3. Corporation

Managed by a board

Definition: A legal entity separate from its owners (shareholders). Shareholders have limited liability.

Key aspects:

  • Separate legal entity from the owners
  • Limited liability for shareholders
  • Managed by a Board of Directors elected by shareholders
  • Must follow strict regulations and registration processes
4. One Person Corporation

One shareholder

Definition: A new business structure in the Philippines introduced in the Revised Corporation Code of the Philippines (RA 11232) in 2019. It is a corporation with one shareholder.

Advantages:

  • Gives the benefits of a corporation, including limited liability, to a single entrepreneur
  • Easier to establish and manage than a traditional corporation
5. Cooperatives

Community-focused organization

Definition: A cooperative is an organization managed by its members with the goal of serving the community and not only earning profit.

6. Cooperatives

These are the cooperative principles, activity types, and related ideas in the notes.

Principles of cooperatives

Seven principles

  • Voluntary and open membership: Open to all who can and want to join
  • Democratic control: One vote per member
  • Member economic participation: Members contribute to capital
  • Autonomy and independence: The cooperative is self-governing
  • Education, training, and information: Training is given to members
  • Cooperation among cooperatives: Working with other cooperatives
  • Concern for community: Caring about community development
Types of cooperative activity

Main forms

  • Credit cooperatives: Lend money to members
  • Consumer cooperatives: Sell products at lower prices
  • Worker cooperatives: The workers own and run the business
  • Multistakeholder cooperatives: Different types of members with different goals

7. Cooperative Development Authority (CDA)

The CDA is the government agency that oversees the implementation of RA 6938 and regulates the establishment of cooperatives in the Philippines.

8. Lesson Insights

Production is the process of turning resources into goods and services in order to meet needs, create jobs, improve living standards, and strengthen competition in the global market.

Entrepreneurs are risk takers who organize resources, create new products or services, and help businesses grow through innovation and decision-making.

Business organizations differ in ownership, management, and liability, while cooperatives focus on community benefit, member participation, and democratic control.

Key ideas to remember: land, labor, capital, entrepreneurship, risk taking, ownership, liability, and cooperation.
