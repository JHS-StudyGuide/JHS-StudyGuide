Economics, Microeconomics & Macroeconomics

A quick side-by-side summary of economics and its two main branches.

Economics

  • The study of how people, businesses, and governments manage scarce resources
  • Deciding what to produce, how to produce it, and who gets it

Microeconomics

  • Looks at the small scale: individual consumers, firms, and markets
  • Studies how supply and demand set prices
  • Explains how people and businesses make choices under limited resources

Macroeconomics

  • Looks at the big scale: entire economies
  • Studies inflation, unemployment, and national income
  • Examines how government spending or monetary policy affects a country as a whole
In short: Microeconomics = individual decisions and markets. Macroeconomics = the economy as a whole.

2. Fathers of Economics

Three economists are traditionally credited as the founders of the field and its two major branches.

1

Adam Smith — Father of Economics

Scottish economist and philosopher whose 1776 work The Wealth of Nations laid the foundations of modern economics, introducing ideas like the division of labor and the "invisible hand" of the free market.

2

Alfred Marshall — Father of Microeconomics

British economist who formalized the study of supply, demand, and price through his 1890 textbook Principles of Economics, establishing the tools still used to analyze individual markets, firms, and consumers.

3

John Maynard Keynes — Father of Macroeconomics

British economist who founded modern macroeconomics with his 1936 work The General Theory of Employment, Interest and Money, arguing that government intervention (spending and monetary policy) can help stabilize an economy, especially during downturns.

3. Maslow's Hierarchy of Needs

Psychologist Abraham Maslow proposed that human needs are arranged in a hierarchy — the lower, more basic levels generally need to be satisfied before a person is motivated by the levels above them. Here they are explained in order, from the base of the pyramid to the top.

1

Physiological Needs

The most basic survival needs: air, food, water, sleep, clothing, and shelter. Until these are met, a person is primarily motivated to satisfy them before anything else.

2

Safety Needs

Once survival needs are met, people seek security and stability — personal safety, employment, health, and access to resources and property.

3

Love and Belonging

The need for social connection: friendship, intimacy, family, and a sense of belonging to a group or community.

4

Esteem Needs

The need for respect and recognition — both self-esteem (dignity, achievement, independence) and esteem from others (status, reputation).

5

Self-Actualization

The top of the hierarchy: realizing one's full potential, pursuing personal growth, creativity, and peak experiences — becoming everything one is capable of becoming.

The levels are usually visualized as a pyramid, with physiological needs at the base and self-actualization at the peak — the idea being that a person generally has to secure the lower levels before the higher ones become a strong motivator.

4. Likas-Kayang Pag-unlad (Sustainable Development)

Kabanata 2: Ang balanseng pagtugon sa mga pangangailangan ng kasalukuyan nang hindi isinasakripisyo ang kakayahan ng susunod na henerasyon na tugunan din ang sarili nilang mga pangangailangan.

Brundtland Report (1987), World Commission on Environment and Development: Sustainable development is development that meets the needs of the present without compromising the ability of future generations to meet their own needs. — Gro Harlem Brundtland, Chair of the UN Brundtland Commission

Ang Kahulugan ng Likas-Kayang Pag-unlad

  • Isang pagtugon nang sapat sa mga pangangailangan at kagustuhan ng kasalukuyang henerasyon
  • Hindi kinokompromiso ang kakayahan ng mga susunod na henerasyon na tugunan din ang kanilang sariling pangangailangan
  • May tatlong dimensyon: ekonomiya, panlipunan, at pangangalaga sa kalikasan
  • Layunin: matugunan ang pangangailangan ng tao sa habang panahon, hindi lang sa kasalukuyan

Ang Pinag-ugatan ng SDGs

  • 2012 United Nations Conference on Sustainable Development, Rio de Janeiro, Brazil — pinag-usapan ng mga kasaping bansa ng UN ang mga suliraning hinaharap ng mundo
  • Setyembre 2015 — muling pinagbuo ang mga layunin sa planong "Transforming Our World: The 2030 Agenda for Sustainable Development" (tinatawag ding "2030 Agenda")
  • Ang 2030 Agenda ay naglalaman ng 17 goals, 169 targets, at 232 indicators, na dapat makamit sa loob ng 15 taon (sa taong 2030)
  • Ang 17 SDGs ay dumaan sa serye ng pambansang konsultasyong dinaluhan ng iba't ibang sektor bago pinagtibay

Sa Pilipinas: Ang AmBisyon Natin 2040 ang pambansang adhikain ng pamahalaan para sa mga Pilipino — ang mas mahaba at mas malawak na plano ng pagpapaunlad ng bansa. Bawat administrasyon ay gumagawa ng sariling Philippine Development Plan (PDP) upang ituloy ang katuparan ng AmBisyon Natin 2040. Ang 2023–2028 PDP ang "medium term development plan" ng administrasyong Marcos, may walong pangunahing pakay na tinatawag na 8-Point Agenda, patungo sa ekonomikong pagbabago para sa isang inclusive at matatag na lipunan na nagtataguyod ng likas-kayang pag-unlad.
