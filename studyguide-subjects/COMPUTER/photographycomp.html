Lesson 01

1. Picture Composition

Definition

What is Picture Composition?

Picture composition is the intentional arrangement of visual elements within a camera frame to convey meaning, balance, and visual appeal.

Framing Rule

The Rule of Thirds

Divides the camera frame into a 3 x 3 grid.

  • Place key subjects on grid lines or intersections.
  • Creates natural balance and an engaging frame structure.
Subject Framing

Framing the Body & Headroom

Proper framing gives portraits a clean, natural look.

  • Headroom: The space left directly above a subject's head.
  • Too much or too little space makes the shot look awkward or cramped.
Color Science

White Balance

Adjusts overall colors so that white objects appear fully white under different lighting conditions.

  • Prevents unwanted color casts (e.g., images appearing too blue or overly orange).
Camera Movement

Reframing

Moving the camera or subject to dynamically improve shot quality.

  • Experiment with different heights and angles.
  • Avoid static or boring default camera setups.

Sources

Natural vs. Artificial Light

  • Natural Light: Sunlight and window light. Soft during morning/evening; harsh at midday.
  • Artificial Light: Lamps, LEDs, studio fixtures, and flashes.
Creative Mood

Using Color Creatively

Colors set mood and visual emphasis within a frame.

  • Warm colors: Evoke energy, passion, or warmth.
  • Cool colors: Express calmness, isolation, or stillness.
  • Contrast highlights key subjects naturally.
Lesson 03

3. Video Controls & Framing

Brightness

Exposure & Brightness

Exposure is the total amount of light captured by the camera sensor.

  • Too bright: Overexposed (clipped highlights).
  • Too dark: Underexposed (loss of shadow detail).
Clarity

Focus Controls

Keeps the subject sharp and clear.

  • Supports both manual and auto-focus options.
  • A blurry subject distracts viewers from the narrative.
Composition

Looking Space

Leave negative space in the direction of the subject's gaze.

  • Prevents a claustrophobic or "trapped" feeling.
  • Creates smooth visual flow across the frame.
Action Framing

Walking Space

Leave space in front of moving subjects.

  • Suggests ongoing forward motion.
  • Prevents a cramped visual cut-off.
Stability

Leveling

Keep the horizon line straight for visual stability.

  • Tilted horizons cause unnecessary viewer distraction.
  • Proper leveling imparts a polished, professional look.
Depth

Background & Foreground

Structure layer depth to isolate your subject.

  • Foreground elements add depth and perspective.
  • Background should support the subject and eliminate distractions.
