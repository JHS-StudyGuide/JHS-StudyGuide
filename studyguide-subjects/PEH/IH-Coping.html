2. Coping Styles

Different approaches people take to manage challenging situations and regulate their emotions.

Emotion-Focused

  • Goal: Reducing the emotions associated with a stressor.
  • Cognitive Approach: Changing thoughts to feel better (e.g., positive thinking, forgiveness).
  • Behavioral Approach: Doing something to feel better (e.g., laughter, meditation, journaling).

Meaning-Focused

  • Goal: Processing and making sense of a situation based on beliefs, values, and goals.
  • Examples: Finding the "good" in a bad situation.
  • Techniques: Thinking positively, using mindfulness.

Social Coping

  • Goal: Seeking emotional or instrumental support from the community.
  • Examples: A child seeking parents, or an adolescent looking for peer support.
  • Actions: Seeking help from a counselor/therapist, or talking with a friend.

Avoidance-Focused

  • Goal: Avoiding the stressor or withdrawing from a stressful situation.
  • Examples: Engaging in distractions to forget problems.
  • Actions: Listening to music, watching TV, shopping, and exercising.

Problem-Focused (Most Effective)

  • Goal: Attempting to control stressors by defining them, planning solutions, and taking action.
  • Impact: Gives the person control over the outcome by addressing the conflict directly.
  • Examples: Creating a study schedule for an exam, talking to a friend to resolve a misunderstanding, seeking professional help to manage finances.

3. Specific Coping Skills

Targeted techniques and actions you can employ immediately during moments of high stress.

1

Self-Soothing

Comforting oneself through the five senses. Ex: Using stress balls, listening to meditation music.

2

Emotional Awareness

Using tools for identifying and expressing feelings. Ex: Journaling, drawing.

3

Mindfulness

Centering and grounding oneself in the present moment. Ex: Breathing exercises.

4

Distraction

Taking the mind off the problem for a while. Ex: Solving puzzles, sewing or crocheting.

5

Opposite Action

Doing something opposite to one's impulse to focus on positive emotions. Ex: Reading a funny book when sad.

6

Crisis Plan

Listing emergency contact info and resources. Ex: Preparing numbers of family, therapists, or emergency teams.
