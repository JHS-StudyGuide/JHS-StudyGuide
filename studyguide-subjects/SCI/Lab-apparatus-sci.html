Labratory & Scientists

Comprehensive overview of labratory rules, apparatuses, and important scientists.

1. Labratory Apparatuses/Equipment

These are equipment, usually glassware or plastic, which are used for experiments and testing while inside the labratory.

  1. Test Tube Brush: Apparatus used to clean out test tubes easily without breaking them.
  2. Erlenmyer Flask: Used to hold liquids to be easily heated. Usually used for liquids that release gas/spatter
  3. Forceps/Tweezers: Handheld instrument used to grip, hold, and manipulate objects. There are two types of tweezers, being bent lips and straight lips.
  4. Beakers: Used to mix, pour, heat, and cool liquids. Not used for measuring liquids.
  5. Ring Stand: Used to hold other apparatus in place, especially useful when heating.
  6. Extension Clamp: Used to secure and extend certain apparatus like the ring stand.
  7. Graduated Cylinder: Like a test tube, used to measure amounts of liquids.
  8. Test Tube: A small cylinder glass apparatus used to hold and observe small liquids.
  9. Medicine Dropper: Used to drop very small amounts of liquid.
  10. Spatula: Used to transfer, mix, and handle small amounts of solid/paste like chemicals.
  11. Stirring Rod: Glass, cylindrical rod used to mix liquids.
  12. Funnel: Conical-shaped, usually plastic material used to channel liquids/powder into other apparatus.
  13. Thermometer: Used to measure the temperature of liquids and solids.
  14. Mortar and Pestle: Apparatus used to grind, crush, and mix chemicals.
  15. Watch Glass: Covers beakers to stop gasses from releasing. Has a small dip in the middle.
  16. pH Meter: Small strip when dipped in liquids shows the alkalinity/acidity of the liquid.
  17. Bunsen Burner: An open glass flame used to heat chemicals and apparatus.
  18. Wire Gauze: Supports glass in heating to slow down the heating proccess.
  19. Tripod Stand: Three legged stand to support apparatus.
  20. Burette: Glassware used for measuring and dropping small amounts of liquid.

2. Labratory Rules

  • Wear proper personal equipment. (Lab coat, gloves, face mask, and goggles.)
  • Use chemicals properly and carefully.
  • For waste disposal, dispose all trash (Solids and liquids) in the proper bins.
  • Always wash hands and eyes if it ever comes in contact with anything.
  • Always be on guard and prepared for emergencies.
  • Label apparatus and chemicals properly.
  • Don't work alone in the labratory unless allowed.
  • Once a chemical of any kind spills, avoid the chemicals and wrap it or wait for personnel to clean it.
  • Strictly no foods or drinks in the labratory unless apart of the experiment.
  • Know proper ethical behaviour ESPECIALLY during experiments.

3. Important Scientists

1. Clara Y. Lim Sylianco (Mutagens and Anti-Mutagens)

Filipino chemist who was granted the title of National Scientist of the Philippines in 1994. Lim-Sylianco's research focused on mutagens, antimutagens, and bio-organic mechanisms. She has published articles, books, and monographs in organic chemistry, biochemistry, genetic toxicology, and molecular nutrition.

2. Dr. Penaloza (Polymer Plastics)

Dr. David P. Penaloza Jr. is a Filipino chemist specializing in polymer chemistry. His research focuses on developing advanced polymer materials, including nanocomposites and environmentally friendly plastics. He has contributed to applications such as water purification, coatings, and high-performance materials. He has published numerous scientific papers and received awards for his contributions to polymer science. His work has helped advance polymer research and technology in the Philippines and internationally.

3. Bienuido O. Juliano (Rice Chemistry)

Bienvenido O. Juliano was a Filipino National Scientist and world-renowned organic chemist who made major contributions to rice chemistry and grain quality research. He spent 32 years at the International Rice Research Institute (IRRI), where his work improved rice quality, nutrition, and breeding methods worldwide. He wrote over 370 scientific papers, mentored many scientists, and received numerous prestigious national and international awards. In recognition of his achievements, he became a member of the National Academy of Science and Technology in 1979 and was named a National Scientist in 2000. He passed away on February 21, 2018, leaving a lasting legacy in agriculture and food science.

4. Luz Oliveros-Belardo (Essential Oils using PH plants)

Dr. Luz Oliveros-Belardo extracted 33 new Philippine essential oils from native plants and studied their chemical and physical properties. Her first research was on the chemical and pharmacological properties of Tanglad Tagalog (Cymbopogon ciratus) and found that its chemicals, such as potassium citrate that is an effective diuretic compound capable of resisting increased blood pressure. She was one of the first Southeast Asians that conducted studies on Chichirica (Vinca rosea) leaves and found that it is rich in alkaloids, glycosides, terpenoids, sterols, fatty acids, and volatile oil.

5. Alfredo Santos (Phaeantharine)

Alfredo is a main pioneer of modern chemistry, discovering phaeantharine, which is a quaternary bisbenzylisoquinoline alkaloid from the native Philippine medicinal plant kalimatas.

6. Marie Curie (Radioactivity)

Marie shared the 1903 Nobel Prize in Physics with her husband, Pierre Curie, "for their joint researches on the radioactivity phenomena discovered by Professor Henri Becquerel". She won the 1911 Nobel Prize in Chemistry "for the discovery of the elements radium and polonium, by the isolation of radium and the study of the nature and compounds of this remarkable element".

7. Alfred Nobel (Nobel Prize, Dynamite)

Nobel's most famous invention, dynamite, was an explosive made using nitroglycerin, which was patented in 1867. He further invented gelignite in 1875 and ballistite in 1887. Upon his death, Nobel donated his fortune to a foundation to fund the Nobel Prizes, which annually recognize those who have "conferred the greatest benefit to humankind". The synthetic element nobelium was named after him.

8. Antione Lavoisier (List of elements, Metric System)

Lavoisier helped construct the metric system, wrote the first extensive list of elements, in which he predicted the existence of silicon, and helped to reform chemical nomenclature. By using more precise measurements than previous experimenters, he confirmed the developing theory that, although matter in a closed system may change its form or shape, its mass always remains the same (now known as the law of conservation of mass), which led to the development of the balanced physical and chemical reaction equations that we still use today.

9. Dmitri Mendeleev (Periodic Table)

Dmitri wrote the periodic table in 1869 while writing a textbook, writing all 63 elements on a card and arranged them until a reoccurring pattern had emerged, arranging elements by Atomic mass and chemical behaviors in vertical columns, and repeating patterns in horizontal rows. Because of gaps in the grid, he accurately predicted the existence, atomic weights, and chemical properties of three undiscovered elements: gallium, scandium, and germanium.
