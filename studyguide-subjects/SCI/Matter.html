Chemistry & Fundamental Principles

Matter, States & Phase Changes

A complete guide on the kinetic particle model, five primary states of matter, and thermal energy transitions.

1. Particle Model of Matter

Core postulates describing the microscopic structure and behavior of all matter.

Kinetic Theory Postulates

Fundamental Properties of Particles

  • Composition: All matter is composed of tiny particles called atoms and molecules.
  • Constant Motion: Particles are in continuous, random motion at all times.
  • Intermolecular Attraction: Particles exert attractive forces on one another.
  • Interparticle Space: There are spaces separating individual particles from each other.
  • Thermal Energy Effect: Heating supplies kinetic energy causing particles to move faster; cooling removes energy causing them to move slower.

2. States of Matter

Physical forms taken by matter depending on thermal energy and particle arrangement.

01 Rigid

Solid

Shape & Volume: Definite shape & definite volume.

Particle Behavior: Particles are tightly packed and only vibrate in fixed positions.
02 Fluid

Liquid

Shape & Volume: Definite volume, but no definite shape (takes shape of container).

Particle Behavior: Particles are close together but slide and bump past each other freely.
03 Diffuse

Gas

Shape & Volume: No definite shape & no definite volume.

Particle Behavior: Particles are far apart and move completely freely in all directions.
04 Ionized

Plasma

Description: Superheated, highly energetic gas-like state.

Particle Behavior: Made of charged particles (ions & free electrons); conducts electricity.
05 Quantum ($T \approx 0\text{ K}$)

Bose–Einstein Condensate (BEC)

Description: Ultra-cold state of matter formed near Absolute Zero ($0\text{ K}$ or $-273.15^\circ\text{C}$).

Particle Behavior: Quantum effects become dominant as individual atoms lose identity and act together like one giant atom.

3. Phase Changes & Thermal Dynamics

Thermodynamic classification of phase transitions based on heat absorption or release.

Endothermic Processes (+Heat)

Processes that absorb thermal energy from surroundings.

  • Melting → Solid to Liquid
  • Evaporation → Liquid to Gas
  • Sublimation → Solid to Gas

Exothermic Processes (-Heat)

Processes that release thermal energy into surroundings.

  • Freezing → Liquid to Solid
  • Condensation → Gas to Liquid
  • Deposition → Gas to Solid

4. Quick Reference Summary Table

A fast review matrix covering physical characteristics and phase transition thermodynamics.

Phase Transition Initial & Final States Thermal Type Particle Dynamics
Melting Solid → Liquid Endothermic Gains energy; breaks rigid lattice to slide past.
Freezing Liquid → Solid Exothermic Loses energy; locks into fixed positions.
Evaporation Liquid → Gas Endothermic Gains energy; overcomes attractive forces to escape.
Condensation Gas → Liquid Exothermic Loses energy; attractive forces draw particles together.
Sublimation Solid → Gas Endothermic Gains energy rapidly; bypasses liquid phase.
Deposition Gas → Solid Exothermic Loses energy rapidly; settles directly into rigid form.
