Math in Chemistry: Significant Figures & Notation

A complete guide covering precision, accuracy, significant figure rules, mathematical operations, and scientific notation.

Significant Figures Number of digits in a measurement that are known with certainty plus 1 estimated digit. Indicates precision.
Accuracy vs Precision Accuracy is closeness to the true value; Precision is consistency among repeated measurements.
Scientific Notation Standard way ($A \times 10^n$) of writing very large and very small numbers while keeping sig figs clear.

1. Fundamental Measurements: Accuracy & Precision

Key definitions establishing how measurements are evaluated in chemical mathematics.

Accuracy

  • Definition: How close a measurement value is to the true or accepted value.
  • Core Concept: Being "close to the correct value."

Precision

  • Definition: How close repeated measurements are to each other.
  • Core Concept: Measurements are consistent with each other.
  • Connection: Significant figures represent the precision of a measurement.

3. Addition, Subtraction, Multiplication & Division

Rules for rounding answers correctly following chemical arithmetic standards.

Addition & Subtraction

Rule: Round your answer to the least number of decimal places.

  • Addition Example:
    2.634 + 0.02 = 2.654
    $\rightarrow$ Rounded to least decimal places (2 decimal places): 2.65
  • Subtraction Example:
    3.09 − 2.0 = 1.09
    $\rightarrow$ Rounded to least decimal places (1 decimal place): 1.1

Multiplication & Division

Rule: Round your answer to the fewest significant figures.

  • Multiplication Example:
    42.3 × 2.61 = 110.403
    $\rightarrow$ 42.3 (3 sf) and 2.61 (3 sf)
    $\rightarrow$ Rounded to 3 sf: 110 (or $1.10 \times 10^2$)

4. Scientific Notation

Standardized representation for extremely large or small quantities that makes significant figures explicit.

Standard Form: $4.1 \times 10^n$ (where $4.1$ is the coefficient and $n$ is the exponent).
