Acids and Bases

Key terms, the litmus paper test, the standard lab method for identifying acids or alkalis, the pH scale, and strong vs. weak acids and bases.

1. Key Terms

Core vocabulary used throughout the topic of acids and bases.

Definitions

Acids, Bases & Related Terms

  • Acid: a solution that has a pH level of less than 7.
  • Alkaline: a solution that has a pH greater than 7.
  • Corrosive substance: a substance that can react with and damage other substances or cause chemical burns to the skin.
  • Base: a substance that reacts with an acid to form a compound called a salt.
  • Litmus paper: used to test whether a solution is acidic or alkaline.
  • Alkalis: soluble bases that form alkaline solutions.
Edible acids have a sour taste, but not all acids are edible.

2. Litmus Paper Test

There are two types of litmus paper — red and blue — and each behaves differently depending on the solution.

A pH < 7

In Acid

Red litmus: stays red.

Blue litmus: turns red.
B pH > 7

In Alkaline Solution

Red litmus: turns blue.

Blue litmus: stays blue.

3. Standard Method for Identifying Acids or Alkalis

The lab procedure used to test a household solution with litmus paper.

Procedure

  • 1. Collect test tubes containing household solutions.
  • 2. Use a pipette to place 3–4 drops of the solution in the well of the spotting tile.
  • 3. Place the ends of pieces of red and blue litmus paper into the solution.
  • 4. Observe your results and record them in your results table.

Equipment

  • Test tube
  • Pipette
  • Spotting tile
  • Test tube rack
  • Litmus paper
  • Household solutions

5. Strong and Weak Acids & Bases

Strength refers to how completely an acid or base ionizes (dissociates) in solution — it is not the same as concentration.

Acids

  • An acid that dissociates 100% into ions is called a strong acid.
  • If an acid does not dissociate 100%, it is a weak acid.

Bases

  • A strong base is a base that is 100% ionized in solution.
  • If a base is less than 100% ionized in solution, it is a weak base.
  • Strong bases are hydroxide (OH⁻) compounds.
