Chemistry & Fundamental Principles

Solutions & Solubility

Understanding how temperature, pressure, and chemical polarity determine how substances dissolve in liquids.

Solubility Definition The maximum amount of solute that can dissolve in a given amount of solvent at a specific temperature.
Solubility Limit Example At 60°C, the solubility of NaCl is about 37g per 100g of water. Any amount beyond this limit remains undissolved.
Core Principle "Like Dissolves Like" — Substances with similar molecular polarities mix and dissolve into one another.

Miscible vs. Immiscible Liquids

When mixing two liquids together, their chemical properties determine whether they blend into a single uniform mixture or separate into distinct layers.

Complete Mixing

Miscible Liquids

Liquids that mix completely in all proportions, forming a homogeneous mixture without separating.

Example: Alcohol + Water
Layer Separation

Immiscible Liquids

Liquids that do not mix uniformly and spontaneously form separate layers when combined.

Example: Oil + Water

Main Factors Affecting Solubility

Three primary physical and chemical variables control how much solute can be dissolved in a given solvent.

1. Solute & Solvent Nature

Determined by molecular polarity ("Like Dissolves Like") and intermolecular forces.

2. Temperature

Affects kinetic energy. Generally increases solid solubility but decreases gas solubility.

3. Pressure

Has minimal effect on solids and liquids, but drastically affects gas solubility in liquids.

Nature of Solute and Solvent: "Like Dissolves Like"

Substances with similar polarity tend to dissolve in each other due to compatible molecular forces.

Solute Type Solvent Type Dissolution Behavior Real-World Example
Polar Polar Dissolves readily Sugar dissolves in water (both are polar)
Nonpolar Polar Does NOT dissolve Oil (nonpolar) does not dissolve in water (polar)
Nonpolar Nonpolar Dissolves readily Grease (nonpolar) dissolves in gasoline (nonpolar)
Ionic Polar Dissolves into individual ions NaCl and NaHCO3 dissolve in water into independent ions.

Temperature Effects on Solubility

Solid Solutes in Liquids

  • For most solid solutes, solubility increases as temperature increases.
  • Some substances (like NaCl) remain relatively constant.
  • A few rare solid substances (like Li2SO4) decrease in solubility as temperature rises.

Gas Solutes in Liquids

  • Gas solubility decreases as temperature increases.
  • Heating gives gas particles more kinetic energy, allowing them to escape from the solution into the vapor phase.
  • Example: Bubbles appear when heating water before it boils because dissolved gases become less soluble.

Individual Compound Trends

Compound Formula Chemical Name Solubility Trend with Increasing Temperature
KNO3 Potassium Nitrate Increases greatly as temperature increases.
KBr Potassium Bromide Increases gradually as temperature increases.
KCl Potassium Chloride Increases gradually as temperature increases.
NaCl Sodium Chloride Changes very little as temperature increases.
Li2SO4 Lithium Sulfate Decreases as temperature increases.

Pressure Effects & Henry's Law

Pressure has almost no noticeable effect on solid and liquid solutes because they are nearly incompressible. However, it strongly influences gas solubility.

Henry's Law

The solubility of a gas in a liquid is directly proportional to the pressure of the gas above the liquid.

  • Higher Pressure → More Gas Dissolves
  • Lower Pressure → Less Gas Dissolves

Real-World Application: Carbonated Beverages

  • Carbon dioxide (CO2) is dissolved in soda under high factory pressure.
  • When the bottle cap is opened, the pressure above the liquid drops rapidly to ambient atmospheric pressure.
  • As pressure drops, CO2 becomes less soluble and escapes from the liquid, producing effervescent fizzing and bubbles.

Summary & Key Takeaways

Quick Review

Key Points Checklist

  • Solid Solubility: Generally increases as temperature increases.
  • Gas Solubility: Decreases as temperature increases (heat causes gas to escape).
  • Pressure Effect: Increasing pressure increases gas solubility in liquids (Henry's Law), but has negligible effect on solids and liquids.
  • Polarity Rule: "Like dissolves like" — substances with matching polarities dissolve in one another.
  • Temperature Dual Effect: Higher temperature helps solids dissolve faster and in greater amounts, but forces dissolved gases out of liquid solutions.
