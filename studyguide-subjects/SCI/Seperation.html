Mixtures & Separation Techniques

A comprehensive review of homogeneous vs. heterogeneous mixtures, solutions, colloids, suspensions, and physical separation methods.

1. Types of Mixtures

Classification based on particle distribution and visibility.

Homogeneous Mixture

  • Distribution: Particles are evenly distributed throughout.
  • Visibility: Uniform composition; individual particles are not visible.
  • Example: Salt water

Heterogeneous Mixture

  • Distribution: Particles are not evenly spread out.
  • Visibility: Particles are visibly noticed; distinct phases are clear.
  • Example: Oil & water (we can clearly see the physical separation)

3. Colloids & Key Properties

Mixtures with medium particle sizes that exhibit distinct optical and kinetic behaviors.

Colloid Characteristics: Particles scatter light, are not easily filtered, and NEVER settle.

Common Examples

  • Milk
  • Toothpaste
  • Fog
  • Blood
  • Smoke

Key Phenomena

  • Tyndall Effect: Scattering of light as a light beam passes through a colloid.
  • Brownian Motion: Colloidal particles move randomly due to constant collisions.
A Formation

Dispersion

Breaking large particles down into smaller, colloidal-sized particles.

Example: Grinding paint
B Formation

Condensation

Smaller particles combine together to form colloidal-sized particles.

Example: Formation of fog

4. Methods of Separating Mixtures

Physical techniques used to isolate components from various types of mixtures.

Separation Techniques

Physical Separation Procedures

  • Sieving / Sifting: Separates solid & solid particles using a mesh screen.
  • Filtration: Separates a solid & liquid mixture using porous material.
  • Evaporation: Separates dissolved salt from liquid through heating.
  • Distillation: Separates liquids with different boiling points.
  • Centrifugation: Separates mixture components by density by spinning at high speed.
  • Magnetic Separation: Uses magnets to extract magnetic materials.
  • Chromatography: Separates components based on different affinities (e.g., separating dyes in ink).
  • Decantation: Separates liquid from a solid or another liquid by carefully pouring it out.
  • Crystallization: Separates substances based on their freezing/cooling point.
