Mixtures, Solutions & Concentration

Comprehensive study guide covering types of mixtures, solution saturation levels, percentage concentrations, worked calculation steps, and matter classification.

1. Mixtures & Classification

Fundamental characteristics and classification of non-chemically combined substances.

Mixture Definition: A combination of 2 or more substances that are not chemically combined. Example: Air is a mixture primarily composed of nitrogen and oxygen.

Homogeneous Mixture (Solution)

  • Uniformity: Uniform composition throughout.
  • Phases: Has 1 visible phase.
  • Visibility: Components are not easily seen.

Heterogeneous Mixture

  • Uniformity: Non-uniform composition.
  • Phases: Has 2 or more visible phases.
  • Visibility: Individual components remain clearly visible.

2. Solutions & Saturation States

Components of a solution and the stages of solute saturation.

Components of a Solution

A solution is formed when one substance is completely dissolved in another.

  • Solute: The substance being dissolved (present in the smaller amount).
  • Solvent: The substance that dissolves the solute (present in the larger amount).

Saturation Levels

  • Unsaturated: Contains less solute than the capacity; below the saturation point.
  • Saturated: Contains the maximum amount of dissolved solute at a given temperature.
  • Supersaturated: Contains more dissolved solute than a saturated solution; above the saturation point.

3. Concentration Formulas

Quantitative expressions for measuring the amount of solute in a given solution.

% m/m Mass / Mass

Mass Percent

$$\text{Mass Percent} = \left( \frac{\text{Mass of Solute}}{\text{Mass of Solution}} \right) \times 100$$
% v/v Volume / Volume

Volume Percent

$$\text{Volume Percent} = \left( \frac{\text{Volume of Solute}}{\text{Volume of Solution}} \right) \times 100$$
% m/v Mass / Volume

Mass/Volume Percent

$$\text{Mass/Volume \%} = \left( \frac{\text{Mass of Solute (g)}}{\text{Volume of Solution (mL)}} \right) \times 100$$

4. Example of a Mass percentage problem.

Step-by-step evaluation of a mass percentage problem.

Sample Problem

Mass % NaCl

Given: $2.35\text{ g NaCl}$ (Solute) and $7.45\text{ g H}_2\text{O}$ (Solvent).

  • Step 1
    $\text{Mass Percent} = \left( \frac{\text{Mass of Solute}}{\text{Mass of Solution}} \right) \times 100$
  • Step 2
    $\text{Mass Percent} = \left( \frac{2.35}{2.35 + 7.45} \right) \times 100$
  • Step 3
    $\text{Mass Percent} = \left( \frac{2.35}{9.80} \right) \times 100$
  • Step 4
    $\text{Mass Percent} = 0.240 \times 100$
  • Final
    24.0% NaCl

5. Properties of Matter

Distinction between physical and chemical characteristics.

Physical Properties

  • Definition: Can be observed or measured without changing the substance's identity.
  • Examples: Color, odor, mass, volume.

Chemical Properties

  • Definition: Describes how a substance reacts to form an entirely new substance.
  • Examples: Rusting, reaction to acids, flammability.

6. Key Comparisons Summary

Quick reference breakdown comparing types of matter and mixtures.

Category Core Distinction Key Attributes Examples
Pure Substance 1 kind of particle Fixed composition; cannot be separated physically. Elements, Compounds
Mixture 2 or more substances Variable composition; can be separated physically. Saltwater, Air
Element 1 type of atom Cannot be broken down chemically. Nitrogen ($\text{N}_2$), Oxygen ($\text{O}_2$)
Compound 2 or more elements Can be broken down chemically. Water ($\text{H}_2\text{O}$), Salt ($\text{NaCl}$)
Homogeneous Uniform composition 1 visible phase. Saltwater
Heterogeneous Non-uniform composition 2 or more visible phases. Oil and water
