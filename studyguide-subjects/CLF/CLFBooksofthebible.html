The Bible and its Categories

A clear study page about how the the Bible is seperated, understood, and divided for basic organization purposes.
The Bible The Bible is a historic document of several writers on the start of the universe all the way to the end.
Old Testament This is the first division of the Bible, holding the books with the creation story all the way to the last prophets.
New Testament This is the second and most important division of the Bible, holding the promises of the messiah.

1. The Categories of the Bible

These are the categories of the Bible, from the old to new testament. The total amount of books is 73. All of the categories of the Bible are of the same importance, no book less important than the other.

1. Old Testament Categories

This is the Old testament, holding 46 books.

  • Torah/Pentateuch

    • These are the "law books", written by Moses before his death. These hold instructions to the Chosen People given directly from God. The Torah holds Genesis, Exodus, Leviticus, Numbers and Deuteronomy

  • Historical

    • These are the historical records of the Bible, showing the formation and designation of the Kings and Judges. This category holds the books from Joshua - 2 Maccabees.

  • Sapiential

    • These are the wisdom books, holding wisdom from the ones chosen by God. These contain profound wisdom given by God. This category holds the books from Job - Sirach/Ecclesiasticus.

  • Prophetic

    • The prophetic books are the books written by the prophets. This category has 2 divisions in itself.

    -- MAJOR

    These are the books that are written by prophets which contributed majorly to the development of God's chosen people. This division contains the books Isiah - Daniel, not including Lamentations since it is written by a prophet.

    -- MINOR

    These are the books that are written by prophets which contributed little to the development of God's chosen people. This division contains the books Hosea - Malachi

2. New Testament Categories

This is the Old testament, holding 27 books.

  • Gospels

    • These are the books commonly referred to as the "Good News". Written by the 4 evangelists Matthew, Mark, Luke, and John, these books refer to the life of Jesus up until his ressurection.

  • Acts (continuation of Luke)

    • This book is a continuation of the Gospel according to Luke, showing how the apostles worked around the world on spreading God's word, the challenges, and the eventual arrival at Rome.

  • Letters/Epistles

    • Seperated under Pauline and General letters, the letters are usually written by St. Paul and are instructions to several kingdoms and people on how to live as a Christian.

  • Revelation/Apocalyptic

    • Revelation, the only apocalyptic book, is the depiction according to John on end of the world and the second coming of Christ, also known as the "Parousia".

Reading during Holy Mass (for more information, go here.)

-- Most Old Testament Books

Say "A reading from the (first/second) Book of _____." Once finished with all of the passage, say "The Word of the Lord."

Example: "A reading from the Book of Genesis."; "A reading from the first (or second) Book of Samuel."

--Prophetic Books

Say "A reading from the Book of the prophet _____." Once finished with all of the passage, say "The Word of the Lord."

Example: "A reading from the Book of the prophet Isaiah."

--Song of Songs

Say "A reading from the Song of Songs. (the only OT text not “from the Book of…”)." Once finished with all of the passage, say "The Word of the Lord."

-- The Gospels

Say "A reading from the holy Gospel according to _____." Once finished with all of the passage, say "The Gospel of the Lord."

Example: "A reading from the holy Gospel according to Luke.";

-- The Letters

Say "A reading from the (first/second) Letter of Paul to the _____." Once finished with all of the passage, say "The Word of the Lord."

Example: "A reading from the Letter of Paul to the Romans."; "A reading from the first (or second) Letter of Paul to the Corinthians."

-- The Letter to the Hebrews

Say "A reading from the Letter to the Hebrews." Once finished with all of the passage, say "The Word of the Lord."

-- Non-Pauline letters

Say "A reading from the Letter of James (or Jude)." Once finished with all of the passage, say "The Word of the Lord."

-- Apocalyptic

Say "A reading from the Book of Revelation. (not plural 'Revelations')" Once finished with all of the passage, say "The Word of the Lord."

3. The 2 Major Divisions of the Bible

This is a deeper dive to the actual message and content of the divisions rather than just categories.

Old Testament

  • The Old Testament tells the story of God's relationship with His people, especially the Israelites. It begins with the creation of the world and the first humans, Adam and Eve. It shows how sin entered the world and affected humanity. Even when people disobeyed Him, God continued to show mercy and gave them opportunities to return to Him. God made covenants with people like Noah, Abraham, Moses, and David. Through these covenants, He promised to guide, protect, and bless His people. The Old Testament also contains God's laws, wisdom, prayers, and the messages of the prophets. These teachings encourage people to live with faith, justice, and obedience to God. The prophets also foretold the coming of a Savior who would bring hope and salvation. Overall, the Old Testament reveals God's love, faithfulness, and plan to redeem humanity.

New Testament

  • The New Testament tells the story of Jesus Christ and the fulfillment of God's promise to send a Savior for humanity. It begins with the birth of Jesus, showing how God became human to bring people closer to Him. Throughout His ministry, Jesus preached about love, forgiveness, repentance, faith, and the coming of the Kingdom of God. He performed many miracles, such as healing the sick, feeding thousands, and raising the dead, revealing His divine power and compassion. Jesus willingly died on the cross to save humanity from sin and restore people's relationship with God. Three days later, He rose from the dead, proving His victory over sin and death and giving believers the hope of eternal life. After His resurrection, Jesus commissioned His disciples to spread the Gospel to all nations, leading to the growth of the early Christian Church. The New Testament also contains letters written by the apostles, which provide guidance on Christian beliefs, worship, moral living, and unity among believers. Finally, the Book of Revelation describes God's ultimate triumph over evil and the promise of a new heaven and a new earth. Overall, the New Testament reveals God's great love, grace, and plan of salvation through Jesus Christ, inviting all people to believe in Him and live according to His teachings.
All of the Bible shares one whole message, that being the coming of the saviour, then the second coming of the same saviour.
