Polygons Quick Guide

1. Classification & Core Terms

Shape Types

  • Convex: no corner "caves in" — every angle is 180° or less, so diagonals stay inside the shape.
  • Concave: at least one corner caves in (angle over 180°), so a diagonal can poke outside the shape.
  • Equilateral: every side is the same length.
  • Equiangular: every angle is the same size.
  • Regular: convex, and both equal sides AND equal angles — the "perfect" version.
  • Irregular: anything that doesn't hit all three regular conditions.

Basic Parts

  • Vertex: a corner — where two sides meet.
  • Interior Angle: the angle you'd measure standing inside the shape, at a corner.
  • Exterior Angle: the angle just outside that same corner, formed by stretching one side past the vertex.
  • Diagonal: a straight line connecting two corners that aren't next to each other.

2. Counting Diagonals

From one corner

Diagonals per Vertex

s - 3

A corner can't connect to itself, or to the two corners right next to it — so subtract those 3.

Whole shape

Total Diagonals (d)

d = [s(s - 3)] / 2

Do that count for every corner, then divide by 2 so you don't count each diagonal twice.

See a worked example

Octagon (8 sides): d = [8 × (8 − 3)] / 2 = [8 × 5] / 2 = 20 diagonals.

Working backward (if d = 54): 54 = [s(s − 3)] / 2 → multiply both sides by 2 → 108 = s² − 3s → s² − 3s − 108 = 0 → factors to (s − 12)(s + 9) = 0 → s = 12 sides (12-sided shapes only, since sides can't be negative).

3. Interior & Exterior Angles

Interior Angles

Sum = (s − 2) × 180°

Any polygon can be cut into triangles from one corner — always (s − 2) of them. Since each triangle is 180°, multiply.

One angle, if regular: m∠i = [(s − 2) × 180°] / s
Exterior Angles

Sum = 360°

Walk all the way around the outside of any convex polygon and you turn exactly one full circle — 360°, no matter the shape.

One angle, if regular: m∠e = 360° / s

4. Area of a Regular Polygon

Area = 1/2 × a × n × s  ≡  Area = 1/2 × a × P

a = apothem (center to the middle of a side) · n = number of sides · s = length of one side · P = perimeter (n × s) — the two formulas are the same thing written two ways.

5. Where This Shows Up in Nature

Fly Eyes & Honeycombs

A regular hexagon has s = 6, so each interior angle works out to:

m∠i = [(6 − 2) × 180°] / 6 = 120°

Three 120° corners fit together with zero gaps (3 × 120° = 360°) — that's why bees build honeycombs out of hexagons and why a housefly's compound eye is packed with hexagonal lenses. It's the most efficient way to tile a surface.
